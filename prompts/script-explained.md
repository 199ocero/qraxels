No research: write from what you already know. Before writing, work out (don't output this separately):
- THE IDEA: the one concept behind the topic, in one plain sentence. If it has a well-known name, who named it and where (a book, a researcher), say so, but only if you're sure.
- THE MECHANISM: WHY it happens, as a short chain of cause and effect. This is the heart of the video.
- THE PICTURE: one concrete analogy or image that makes the mechanism obvious (an ice cube warming one degree at a time, a seed underground, a loading bar stuck at 99%). Use it more than once: set it up, return to it, pay it off at the end.
- THE EXAMPLE: one or two everyday situations the viewer has lived through, with specifics (three weeks at the gym, ten posts with twelve views).
- WHAT TO DO: 2–3 practical, concrete steps that follow from the mechanism. Things a person could do tonight.
- THE LIMIT: where the idea stops being true, said honestly.

Tag every beat with its section, in this order:
1. hook (1–2 lines): a question or a situation the viewer recognizes in themselves, in the first three seconds. It must make them want the answer. Never open with the concept's name or "Have you ever wondered".
2. context (2–3 beats): the situation in plain words. Who this happens to, what it looks like from the inside, why it matters. Set up the picture here.
3. idea (2–4 beats): name the idea, then explain the mechanism step by step. "Here's what's actually going on." This is where the music comes in.
4. example (1–3 beats): the picture and the everyday example, played out so the mechanism is obvious.
5. how (2–4 beats): what to do. Number the steps out loud ("One: ... Two: ...") so they're easy to follow.
6. caveat (1 beat): the honest limit, in one or two sentences.
7. close (1–2 beats): return to the hook or the picture, now understood differently. The last line is the one they'll remember, and it ends with a full stop.

Clarity rules (the most important part):
- Every line follows from the line before it. Never mention something that hasn't been set up: no "the spreadsheet", "Column B" or "that number" unless the viewer already knows what it is.
- Define every term the first time you use it, in plain words.
- Plain English a 12-year-old could follow. Sentences of 6–20 words. One idea per sentence.
- Signpost: "Here's the problem.", "So why does this happen?", "Here's the trick.", "So what do you do?"
- Say "you" and talk to one person. Calm, warm, a little curious. Mostly "." and "?"; no "!" except at most once.
- Before you finish, read it as someone who has never heard of this idea. If any line would make them ask "wait, what?", rewrite it.

Content rules:
- Up to 3 facts, numbers or named sources, only ones you're sure are true; list every one in flagged_claims. No made-up studies, no "studies show" without saying which, no statistics lists.
- No get-rich promises, no financial or medical advice, no fake life story ("when I was broke...").
- No hype vocabulary: "grind", "hustle", "level up", "unlock your potential", "journey", "comfort zone", "no excuses", "nobody is coming to save you".
- Each beat = 1–3 sentences + a one-line idea of what's on screen. Think in OBJECTS from this topic: a line chart, a calendar, a plan being ticked off, a tree growing, a jar filling, a door, a bridge built plank by plank, an hourglass, a puzzle coming together.

Bold black-and-white line art: shapes, silhouettes, diagrams, arrows; no maps of many places, crowds of detail or long text.
- No two beats share a picture. When the close returns to the hook's picture, show it changed (now finished, now understood), not a copy.
- HARD LIMIT: 210–270 words of narration in total. Count them. It's read calmly, about 2.25 words a second with the pauses, so this fills 95–120 seconds. Over 270 runs past two minutes.
