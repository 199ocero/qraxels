Write ONE speech that makes someone put their phone down and go work, AND leaves them understanding why. It should feel like a coach in a locker room who first tells you the truth about what's happening to you, calmly and clearly, then fires you up: the hype only hits if the viewer understood the reason for it. Not a lecture, not a pep talk with nothing inside it.

Build the speech on that story. The SHAPE is a wave, not a wall: quiet, building, a dip, the drop, a peak, then a warm landing that loops back to the first line. Contrast is what makes the loud parts hit. A speech that's loud the whole time feels flat, and one that stops at its loudest feels cut off.

Tag every beat with its section, in this order (the angle decides the words; never fill these slots with stock lines):
1. hook (1 line, under 8 words): a pattern interrupt in the first second. Start mid-story or mid-thought ("It's 2:14 AM and the tab is still open."), a contradiction, or a strange specific image tied to the angle. Said at talking volume, close to the mic: curiosity, not shouting. It must leave a question open. If it could open any motivation video, it's wrong.
2. story (4–7 lines): story-time, raw and specific, like telling a friend the truth at 2 AM: the START, the SLIP, the QUIT, in second person. Conversational, building slowly. Every object or number gets introduced before it's referred to ("You open a spreadsheet to track your side income." before "Column B"). End it with an open loop: one line that promises something still to come, and pay it off later.
3. dip (3–6 lines): the quietest, heaviest moment, and where the viewer finally UNDERSTANDS. Name THE IDEA and explain WHY it happens in plain words, through THE PICTURE (why the effort didn't show, why quitting felt reasonable). Then the COST, made physical. Room to breathe. The breath before the drop.
4. turn (1–2 lines): everything flips: now that you know why, the OTHER YOU. The beat drops here.
5. rise (8–12 lines): the loudest part. Commands that escalate, and each one is a concrete thing to do that follows from THE IDEA (not just "go!"); pick ONE fresh action word and hammer it, each repeat raising the stakes; lines get shorter toward the end. Put ONE sudden quiet line in the middle (3–5 words, almost whispered, the truest thing in the speech), then explode again. That contrast is what makes it feel alive.
6. peak (1 line): the line they'll screenshot. It answers the hook's question and pays off the open loop.
7. landing (2–3 lines): come down like the last shot of a film, not a power cut. Slower, warmer, almost smiling. Return to the hook's exact image or object, now changed. The LAST line must flow straight into the FIRST line when the video loops (write it so "<last line> <first line>" reads as one thought), and still feel finished on its own: a full stop, not a question, not "let's go", not "follow for part 2".

Craft rules:
- Dynamics through punctuation (the narrator copies it into her delivery): hook, story and landing end in "." (talking volume); the dip may use "..."; "!" only in the turn, the rise and the peak. Questions are sharp and answered instantly.
- Clarity first: every line follows from the line before it. Never mention a thing, person or number that hasn't been set up. Before finishing, read it as someone who has never heard of this idea; if any line would make them ask "wait, what?", rewrite it.
- Rise and peak lines are 1–8 words. Story, dip and landing lines may run up to 15 words so there's room to explain. One idea per line. Fragments are good in the rise. Vary line lengths like a drum fill.
- Tools: contrast pairs, a chant that escalates, a question slammed shut with a one-word answer, "you" everywhere, an open loop that pays off. Use each tool with your OWN words; invent new pairs and chants for this topic.
- Concrete, specific images beat abstract words: a real place, a real time, a real object from this topic. Never "discipline", "grind", "success" on their own.
- Banned (overused on this channel and everywhere): "nobody is coming to save you", "nobody cares", "get up" chains, "every single day", "someone hungrier", "I'll start Monday", "here's what you're going to do", "rest isn't weakness", "rise and grind", "hustle harder", "level up", "unlock your potential", "journey", "comfort zone", "no excuses".
- Swearing is allowed when it lands harder, at most 2 times, never as filler. No slurs, no insults aimed at any group, nothing about self-harm.
- Up to 3 facts, numbers or named sources (a book, a researcher), only ones you're sure are true; list every one in flagged_claims. No biographies, no statistics lists, no compound-interest math, no "studies show" without saying which.
- No get-rich promises, no financial advice, no fake life story ("when I was broke…").
- Each beat = 1–3 lines + a one-line idea of what's on screen. Think in OBJECTS from this topic, not workouts: a line chart crashing or climbing, a stack of cash growing, a whiteboard plan getting ticked off, a car driving past a highway sign, a phone feed scrolling forever, a calendar with days crossed out, a door opening into light, a wall built brick by brick, a clock, a crowd walking past, a mountain summit. Gym images (pull-ups, deadlifts, punching bag, sprinting) at most once in the whole speech.
- HARD LIMIT: 240–320 words of narration in total. Count them. It's delivered fast with short pauses, so this fills 90–120 seconds.
